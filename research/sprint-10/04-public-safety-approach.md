# Mayoral Record . Policy Scenario Card . Card 4 of 5: Public safety approach

## Mission

I'm doing research for a civic-transparency site, The Mayoral Record (mayoralrecord.com), covering the Toronto 2026 mayoral race. We're publishing a structured "Policy Scenario" card on a contested topic. Your job is to produce the full structured data for one card. Every claim carries a citation. No fabrication. If you can't find a real citation for a claim, drop the claim.

## Candidates (current field; more entrants expected)

- **Brad Bradford.** City Councillor, Beaches-East York. Running on private-sector primary, fiscal restraint, accelerated approvals.
- **Olivia Chow.** Incumbent Mayor of Toronto. Running on public investment, city-as-developer, progressive revenue tools.

## Source-tier system. Tag every citation with its tier.

- **T1**: Primary government data. City of Toronto budget, Auditor General, Statistics Canada, CMHC, planning department, federal/provincial budget docs.
- **T2**: Independent analysis. Wellesley Institute, IMFG, OECD, CHRA, Toronto Region Board of Trade, Conference Board, BIA-commissioned studies, investigative journalism with traceable primary sources.
- **T3**: Peer-reviewed academic. DOI'd journal articles only.
- **T4**: In-house extrapolation. Use rarely. Each T4 claim requires an explicit methodology paragraph.

## Editorial constraints

- No em dashes anywhere. Use periods, commas, colons, or parentheses.
- Evidence-based language. "Research on X incidence finds Y%" not "X benefits group Z."
- Both candidates analyzed under the same framework. Ask the same questions of each mechanism.
- When literature is mixed or thin, say so explicitly. Do not publish a singular numerical projection unless the literature supports a confident plural range.
- Real citations only. Real authors, real years, real DOIs/URLs. Verify before including.
- Avoid: "always," "never," "obviously," "clearly," "of course."

## Topic

How the city allocates effort between policing, social services, harm reduction, and prosecutorial focus on safety outcomes. Bradford's emphasis on policing and enforcement vs Chow's emphasis on community investment, mental health response, and harm reduction.

## Comparable jurisdictions to consider (add or substitute if better-evidenced)

- **Vancouver Downtown Eastside (DTES)**. Harm reduction outcomes. Supervised consumption sites. Overdose mortality and morbidity data. Police interaction trends.
- **Eugene, Oregon (CAHOOTS)**. Unarmed crisis response model. Cost data, outcome data, transferability analysis.
- **Glasgow Violence Reduction Unit**. Public-health framing of violence reduction. Long-run homicide outcomes vs control jurisdictions.
- **Camden, New Jersey**. Police restructuring (2013 disbanding and rebuild). Crime outcomes, community trust outcomes.
- **Calgary CPS partnership programs**. Community-policing outcomes data. Recent partnership models with social services.

Pick 3 to 5 with the strongest documented outcomes and clearest applicability to Toronto. Caveats (legal regime, scale, time-period mismatch) must be made explicit.

## Output format. Produce in Markdown.

### TOPIC
One-line statement, plain English.

### SLUG
URL-safe slug (lowercase-with-dashes).

### PULL QUOTE
1 to 3 sentences. This appears in chat receipts and as og:description. Evidentiary, not advocacy.

### WHO BENEFITS

#### Bradford's mechanism
2 to 4 sentences. Incidence research. Who reaches what, on what timeframe. Caveats explicit.

#### Chow's mechanism
2 to 4 sentences. Same framework. Same questions.

#### Literature
Inline citation row with tiers. Format: "Author on topic (Tier, year) . Author on topic (Tier, year) . ..."

### POSITIONS

#### Brad Bradford
1 to 2 sentences on the actual policy. Cite platform doc, council motion, or public statement with date and outlet.

#### Olivia Chow
1 to 2 sentences. Same format.

### STATUS QUO
What's actually happening in Toronto now. Numerical where possible. Citations.

#### Existing policy stack
Bullet list of programs already in place that a reader should weigh further proposals against. Each item cited.

### COMPARABLE JURISDICTIONS
3 to 5 jurisdictions. For each:
- Name and time period
- Summary (2 to 3 sentences) of the enacted policy
- Outcome (numerical where possible) with citations and tier
- Caveats made explicit

### PROJECTIONS
If literature supports a confident plural range, produce 2 to 3 plural projections with citations. Otherwise, write: "Literature does not support a confident singular projection on this question. The comparable-jurisdiction outcomes above are the closest defensible numerical anchors."

### TIME HORIZON
1 sentence on when effects materialize (year 1? years 3 to 7? decades?). Cite if possible.
