Crime trends

## PULL QUOTE
The "everywhere, all the time" framing collides with the 2025 data. Toronto Police Service Major Crime Indicators are down across the board, auto theft is off its 2023 peak by roughly 40 percent, and homicides are tracking the lowest level in close to four decades. Statistics Canada's Crime Severity Index for the Toronto CMA is among the lowest of any major Canadian metro.

## RECEIPT INTRO
Toronto crime peaked in 2023 on most major indicators and has fallen sharply through 2024 and 2025. The data does not match the "everywhere, all the time" framing for 2025, though several categories (assault, shootings) remain elevated relative to 2018 baselines.

## EXHIBITS

### Exhibit 1
- **Sub-section anchor (URL fragment):** auto-theft-trend
- **Sub-claim:** Auto theft trend in Toronto, 2018 to 2025
- **Finding:** Auto theft surged from 2021 to a 2023 peak, then fell roughly 23 percent in 2024 and an additional decline in 2025. Year to date 2025 totals are roughly 43 percent below the 2023 peak.
- **Metric:** 2023: 12,501 reported auto thefts (peak). 2024: 9,570 (down ~23 percent). 2025 (through Dec. 21): 7,044 (tracking ~44 percent below 2023 peak).
- **Source:** T1, Toronto Police Service Public Safety Data Portal, Auto Theft dashboard, retrieved via TPS-cited reporting in 2025. https://data.torontopolice.on.ca/pages/auto-theft
- **Caveats:** 2024 and 2025 totals remain well above 2014 to 2019 levels (which sat below 4,000 per year). The decline is real, the absolute level is still elevated.
- **As of:** 2025-12-26

### Exhibit 2
- **Sub-section anchor (URL fragment):** homicide-trend
- **Sub-claim:** Homicide trend in Toronto, 2018 to 2025
- **Finding:** 2024 homicides hit an elevated 84 to 86 level. 2025 reversed that sharply, finishing at the lowest reported homicide count in roughly four decades.
- **Metric:** 2018: 96 (per TPS Annual Statistical Report). 2023: 73. 2024: 84 to 86. 2025 (through Dec. 30): 42 (lowest since 1986, per TPS-cited reporting).
- **Source:** T1, Toronto Police Service Homicide data, https://www.tps.ca/data-maps/data-analytics/homicide/ . T2 (corroborating): Global News, "Toronto homicides on pace for record low," 2025-12. https://globalnews.ca/news/11573350/toronto-homicide-rate-drops/
- **Caveats:** Year-over-year homicide counts are volatile in any single city. Multi-year averages are more stable. 2024's spike shows the noise.
- **As of:** 2025-12-30

### Exhibit 3
- **Sub-section anchor (URL fragment):** shootings-trend
- **Sub-claim:** Shooting incidents in Toronto, 2023 to 2025
- **Finding:** Shootings rose sharply from 2023 to 2024, then fell sharply in 2025. The 2025 total is below the 2023 baseline.
- **Metric:** 2023: 345 shooting incidents. 2024: 461 incidents (43 deaths, 120 injuries). 2025 (through Dec. 21): 257 incidents (19 deaths, 98 injuries), a year-over-year drop of ~43 percent.
- **Source:** T1, Toronto Police Service Public Safety Data Portal, Shootings dashboard, https://data.torontopolice.on.ca/ . T2 (corroborating): CP24, "Toronto saw a rash of brazen crime in 2024," 2025-01-02. https://www.cp24.com/local/toronto/2025/01/02/toronto-saw-a-rash-of-brazen-crime-that-shocked-and-unnerved-residents-last-year-is-the-city-still-safe/
- **Caveats:** Shooting victims (deaths plus injuries) is a separate metric from incident count and tells a slightly different story.
- **As of:** 2025-12-21

### Exhibit 4
- **Sub-section anchor (URL fragment):** crime-severity-index
- **Sub-claim:** Toronto's Crime Severity Index relative to Canada
- **Finding:** Toronto CMA's Crime Severity Index is well below the national CSI and is among the lowest of major Canadian metros. The CSI ticked down 1 percent year over year in 2024.
- **Metric:** Toronto CMA CSI 2024: 59.4 (down 1 percent from 2023). Toronto CMA crime rate 2024: 4,177 per 100,000 (down 1 percent). Canada CSI 2024: 77.9 (down 4.1 percent).
- **Source:** T1, Statistics Canada, "Police-reported Crime Severity Index and crime rate, by census metropolitan area, 2024," released 2025-07-22. https://www150.statcan.gc.ca/n1/daily-quotidien/250722/t004a-eng.htm
- **Caveats:** The CSI weights offences by severity but does not capture fear of crime, victim experience, or under-reported categories.
- **As of:** 2025-07-22

### Exhibit 5
- **Sub-section anchor (URL fragment):** mci-aggregate
- **Sub-claim:** Major Crime Indicators (assault, auto theft, robbery, B&E, theft over) aggregate trend
- **Finding:** Total MCI counts rose from 2021 to a 2024 peak, then fell in 2025. As of late December 2025, total MCIs were tracking roughly 12 percent below the 2024 full-year total.
- **Metric:** 2021: 37,926 MCIs. 2024: 51,006 MCIs (full year). 2025 (through Dec. 21): 45,146 MCIs. Mayor's office cites year-over-year category declines: shootings down 43.1 percent, auto theft down 31 percent, break-ins down 12.5 percent, robberies down 16.2 percent.
- **Source:** T1, Toronto Police Service Major Crime Indicators dashboard, https://data.torontopolice.on.ca/pages/major-crime-indicators . T2 (corroborating): ProtectionPlus aggregation of TPS data, published 2025-12-26. https://www.protectionplus.ca/resources/blog/toronto-crime-statistics/
- **Caveats:** MCI excludes sexual assault. Aggregate counts are sensitive to reporting practices and population growth.
- **As of:** 2025-12-21

### Exhibit 6
- **Sub-section anchor (URL fragment):** national-context
- **Sub-claim:** National crime context, 2024
- **Finding:** Statistics Canada reported a 4 percent national decline in CSI in 2024, the first decrease after three years of increases. Motor vehicle theft fell 17 percent nationally and breaking and entering fell 11 percent. Toronto's trajectory is consistent with that broader Canadian pattern, not a Toronto-specific surge.
- **Metric:** Canada CSI 2024: 77.9, down 4.1 percent from 2023. National motor vehicle theft: down 17 percent. National B&E: down 11 percent.
- **Source:** T1, Statistics Canada, "Police-reported crime statistics in Canada, 2024," The Daily, released 2025-07-22. https://www150.statcan.gc.ca/n1/daily-quotidien/250722/dq250722a-eng.htm
- **Caveats:** National declines do not negate the lived-experience concerns of victims or specific Toronto neighbourhoods with elevated counts.
- **As of:** 2025-07-22

## WHAT DATA CANNOT SETTLE
The data does not measure subjective fear of crime, neighbourhood-level erosion of trust, or the disproportionate impact of high-profile incidents (home invasions, jewellery store robberies, transit assaults) on perception. October 2025 polling showed 32 percent of Torontonians naming crime the top issue and 72 percent believing homicide had increased over the past year, even as it fell to a near 40-year low. Receipts can correct numbers. They cannot, on their own, repair perception.

## COMPARABLES
Toronto CMA's 2024 CSI of 59.4 sits well below the national CSI of 77.9 and is consistently among the lowest of large Canadian CMAs. Statistics Canada notes Toronto is among the six CMAs with the consistently lowest violent crime rates over recent years.
